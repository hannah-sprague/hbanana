As a totally unbiased former Brown College Membership Co-chair, I think the community-driven nature of our application process is pretty neat. Written, anonymized, and scored by residents, apps turn out... unique, to say the least.

The deadline was June 1 for applicants, so we just recently (a week ago, ish) finished our summer application reading cycle. I had a lot of fun with it! There were a couple of really fantastic, high-effort ones in my batch.

Since this is the void anyhow (and, like, I followed instructions and didn't share any identifying information), I figure I might as well show you a couple favorite parts of my application from last year when I applied to Brown. It's definitely very me.
<br>

**FREE SPACE**

> Dear Brown College Membership Team,
> I only have one thing to say here, and it’s far more important than anything else I could tell you in this space. 
> If you admit me, I will make you this cake.

![Image]({{"images/bribery_cake.jpg",  | relative_url }})

> Now, this may look like merely a brown rectangle, but don’t be fooled. I personally guarantee you that this chocolate sheet cake is the best cake you will ever try. This cake has never lasted a single day, no matter how few people are around to eat it. And it’s one-of-a-kind: as far as I’m aware, there’s only one physical copy of the recipe in the world, written in my grandmother’s difficult-to-read cursive. 
> Trust me, you don’t want to miss out on this. Don’t think of it as bribery, think of it as a ✨potential expression of gratitude ✨. A delicious one.
> I don’t know how you could not admit me with so much on the line, so… I’ll just trust that I will see you in the fall. Please let me know what days of the week would be most convenient for your team to receive a secret cake delivery. :)

> With Love,
> You-Told-Me-Not-To-Reveal-My-Name

> Also, I did send this to some actual college admissions committees after I got waitlisted.

**Question 3 – CREATIVITY**

A. You get to make one completely benign thing illegal. What is it, what is the punishment, and how is it enforced?

> The thing I am making illegal is not benign at all, but for some reason society has not yet recognized its perniciousness. That is, naming one’s child Aiden.
> 
> Have you ever met an Aiden who wasn’t an absolute menace to society? No. They don’t exist. When a new Aiden is born, the devil marks him immediately. It matters not how well-intentioned and misinformed the child’s parents are when naming him, the Aiden will turn out evil. This effect can be seen most clearly in twin studies; if two identical twins are born and one is named Aiden while the other is not, the Aiden is much more likely to become a bully, criminal, and all-around terrible person. Meanwhile, the non-Aiden child can turn out perfectly well-adjusted even despite the wicked influences of living with someone named Aiden.
> 
> It is, therefore, for the wellbeing of society as a whole that I am making it illegal to name one’s child Aiden.
> 
> Now, in the cases where parents choose to break the law and name their child Aiden anyways, it is not enough to merely change the child’s legal name– the damage has been done. The only solution to enforce this law is to take the child on precisely their twelfth birthday and force them through an exorcism. This tends to be a traumatic experience for all those involved, especially the exorcist who has to endure the presence of an Aiden for several hours, but is the only way to rectify the situation. 
> 
> Meanwhile, parents that choose to inflict such a danger upon the world will be punished by having to become exorcists themselves. Additionally, upon death, they will certainly take their places among the devil’s court in hell. No further punishment is needed by the state.
> 
> It is my sincerest hope that by enacting this law we will forever rid the world of new Aidens, but evil is a constant force that must be fought against. This is but one step in the right direction.


**QUESTION 4 - WHY BROWN?** 

> I thought about taking this question as unseriously as the rest, but I think it’s important that, for the first time on this application, I am completely straightforward with you.
> All in all, I’m absolutely thrilled to go to UVA. I’m so excited. But (and this would be my worry about any college really) I am worried about finding “my people.” People who aren’t going to scoff at me when I suggest a board game night or get excited about a cool physics problem.
> Two years ago, when asked why I, a self-described “math person” with no art skills to be heard of, was taking IB Visual Arts, my legitimate answer (I swear I’m not making this up– it’s in my sketchbook from the first day’s assignment) was “I’m on a quest to become interesting.” I’m not sure if I’ll ever actually say that I’ve achieved that goal, but the quest will forever be ongoing.
> I want to live in Brown not to find a bunch of people who have the exact same interests as me– I’m just looking for people who think it’s cool to care about things. Brown being for “the interesting and interested–” that sounds like a place for the type of person I want to surround myself with and the type of person I want to be like.
