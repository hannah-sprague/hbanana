Writing this in Obsidian 'cause it's native Markdown (which is what this Jekyll site uses) and because I've had enough with Google Docs pushing Gemini on me. AI's super useful for coding, I won't deny it, but c'mon, my writing is *mine*. You can't take my voice away from me, Google.

Continuing with my semi-Luddite trend here, I recently caved and bought a print subscription to The New Yorker. Sometimes I just need to feel the paper in my hands and read from a surface that doesn't glow. Hopefully I'll be able to keep up with its weekly publication pace during the year... I have my doubts.

Content-wise, I enjoy the coverage and writing of The New Yorker. I think some (not all) of the essays take a bit of a judgy or elitist stance (Louis Menand's *Live and Learn* comes to mind... sorry Casteen </3), but there's always substance to chew on. Might I disagree with some of it? Sure. It's at least *good*. Sure as hell better than anything I could write. 

Anytime I read something I like, I want to try and mimic its style. Over the course of reading the current New Yorker issue, I had multiple ideas (and even one outline) for essays I thought I could write in here in response to things I'd read. After reading "[The Billionaires’ Vagina Club](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/07/06/the-billionaires-vagina-club)" (about an expensive Silicon Valley concierge gynecologist and her services) and "[Regression to the Mien](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/07/06/the-human-face-a-cultural-history-fay-bound-albert-book-review)" (about modern faces and technology), I thought I had a real point to make about how, when we try to "optimize" an aspect of our lives or a larger system (health, efficiency, beauty, success, etc.), we always pay an opportunity cost. I had a clever title and everything: "Losing the Forest for the Tallest Possible Trees." I was pretty proud of that! Then I read the next article in the issue, "[Die Another Day](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/07/06/morbid-saul-justin-newman-book-review-eat-your-ice-cream-ezekiel-j-emanuel)," and it covered everything I wanted to say in the succinct conclusion of its way-more-interesting discussion of how most really old (like, statistical-anomaly old) people are actually more likely to just have a birth certificate error or the like. So you got this appreciation-ramble instead of a boring and ultimately probably common sense essay.

As seems to be kind of my thing at this point, here's a small, not-evenly-sampled collection of quotes I liked from the issue:

"One year, Mark tried fasting, but he told me he had to stop because he found it made him really hungry."

"When I got off the phone with Mark, I always said 'I love you, you're a gorgeous person.' I did, and do, and he was."
(Both of those from Ian Frazier's "Postscript" on his colleague Mark Singer)

"Bing Thomas spoke out of the corner of his new mouth. 'Look who deshided to show up.'"
From Will Mackin's "Pig Lab"

"'Kick it like how you would kick a human,' Pathak said.
'I wouldn't kick a human,' I said."
From Stephen Witt's "Are Humanoid Robots Ready to Be Deployed?"
