As a big fan of beans, soup, and spicy food, I find this soup the perfect thing to make on Sunday and eat throughout the week. It's filling and makes me feel all warm and happy inside.

I originally got the recipe from [The Pioneer Woman](https://www.thepioneerwoman.com/food-cooking/recipes/a70222836/hearty-black-bean-soup-recipe/ "https://www.thepioneerwoman.com/food-cooking/recipes/a70222836/hearty-black-bean-soup-recipe/"), but I made some changes based on what I had available and what I know I like. 

Recommended listening while you make this soup: Nina Simone's wonderful album [A Single Woman](https://open.spotify.com/album/5pSGlGB1DVrSlvQBcvlPyB?si=d08c8711f2d8463b)
(I also recommend dancing around your kitchen while it simmers.)

Ingredients:
- 1lb dried black beans (you have to soak them but they're better and cheaper, trust)
- 1lb uncooked bacon
- 1 red bell pepper
- 2 serrano peppers (note: do NOT touch your face after pulling the seeds out. you would think this would be common sense... definitely not learned the hard way)
- 1 or 1.5 yellow onions
- 1 carton chicken broth (low sodium if you want to control the salt level yourself; the bacon already adds a fair bit)
- 1 lime
- 1 bunch of cilantro
- 5 cloves garlic
- spices
	- salt & msg
	- paprika
	- cumin
	- red pepper flakes
	- oregano
	- chili powder
	- bay leaves
	- any other spices you feel like adding

Instructions:
1. Since we're starting from dried beans, you do have to soak them in water overnight. Apparently this is important for making sure the texture is consistent & helps get rid of things that are hard for humans to digest. Do that & drain when you're ready 
2. Slice the bacon into small pieces and fry it up in a pot big enough for your entire soup. I used my beloved dutch oven.
3. While the bacon simmers, you're gonna do all your chopping: dice the peppers and onions, mince the garlic and cilantro (the stem is fine to put in the soup too if you chop it finely enough, but I prefer to only include about half of the stems), and wedge your lime. 
4. Once the bacon is cooked, scoop it out into a bowl for now and drain most of the grease into a jar.
5. In the remaining bacon grease, sauté the garlic, peppers, and onion. Once they're sufficiently cooked (i.e. not crunchy anymore), add your spices and cook for a few more minutes. You can do the spices however you want, pretty much. For me, I wanted to avoid my soup tasting like chili so I went light on the chili powder in favor of paprika, and heavier on the cumin. I was pretty sparing on the salt since there's already chicken broth and bacon in the recipe, and generous with the red pepper flakes because I like it spicy. Do with that as you will.
6. Add your beans, chicken broth, and most of the cilantro. (Save a little for topping if making your bowl of soup look pretty makes you happy like it does for me.) Simmer mostly covered for 1.5 to 2 hours or until the beans are completely soft. If you're feeling like "eh they're a little crunchy but I don't mind" see [this reddit thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/Cooking/comments/rtcjua/i_poisoned_my_wife_and_her_family_on_new_years/) and then if you still want to eat your crunchy beans, knock yourself out.
7. At this point, the original recipe recommends blending some of the beans for a thicker texture. I'm way too lazy to clean a blender, so I used the wooden spoon I'd been occasionally stirring to mush some of the beans against the side of the pot, and that seemed to be just fine.
8. At this point, if you're like me, you are likely very hungry because you started your soup at a kinda reasonable dinner time and now it's like two hours later and your soup is finally ready. Scoop yourself a bowl and top with some fresh cilantro and the juice from one of those lime wedges. Bon appetit!
